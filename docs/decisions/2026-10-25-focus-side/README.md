# Which side first: customer concierge or shop platform (council decision, 2026-10-25)

Status: **working pick, Option 1 (customer-side concierge with a supplier portal), confidence 55/100**, pre-registered
reversal rule, no side-specific code before the week 1 shop survey is read. Founders (Ban, Gabrielle) decide; this
document is the evidence and the argument.

How this was produced: two evidence packs were compiled first (`evidence-internal.md`: repo, deck and code facts
with file:line pointers; `evidence-market.md`: precedents, PH market, price-certainty and commission research with
sources and confidence grades). A council of five agents then debated on those packs only: an advocate for each
option, a CFO and unit-economics skeptic, an operations realist, and an incubator coach / seed investor. Each wrote
an opening statement, cross-examined the other four, and restated a position with a confidence number. A judge
ruled; two red teams (evidence lens, founder-reality lens) attacked the ruling; the judge answered every objection
and issued the final ruling below. Raw transcript: 14 agent outputs, kept outside the repo.

## Ban's original argument, tested
"Go customer-side so the customer knows the cost up front; shop-side means random costs after weigh-in." The
council upheld the direction and narrowed the claim: a brand-set bag price gives a **bounded** bill (base bag plus a
published per-kg step, the Laundryheap shape), not a deleted surprise. Option 2 can approximate bill certainty with
a hard cap, the deferred B7 approval step and a scale photo, so price control is a lever Option 1 holds more
tightly, not one Option 2 lacks. The arguments that actually carried were different: Option 2 has no payer other
than the household (PH shop software is going free), the panel's "one side" is only honoured by a seller-with-vendors
shape, and every surviving concierge brand sets the customer price and outsources the wash.

## Final ruling (judge, after red team)

## Question
The AZUL Hub panel told the founders that serving both households and laundry shops is too much for a pre-launch team of three and asked them to pick one side. Option 1 makes Wash & Go the brand the household buys from, with one published price and partner shops paid wholesale. Option 2 makes Wash & Go the shops' booking, logistics and payout tool, with shops setting prices and the bill following the shop's weigh-in.

## Recommendation
Option 1 in a defined shape: a customer-side concierge with a supplier portal. One published wash-dry-fold price in the Laundryheap form (a base bag at the 6 kg planning size plus a published per-kg step at or above the wholesale rate), delivery shown as the code's published matrix (₱40 within 2 km, ₱8 per km after, capped at ₱150), the ₱7 service fee as its own line, one or two partner shops paid on their recorded weigh-in at a negotiated wholesale rate, and the portal kept as the supplier's weigh-in and invoice tool. The household is the only party Wash & Go sells to. The pilot runs on the Express booking and dispatch path that exists, because the Scheduled tier is a booking skeleton with no batching or dispatch code. Confidence 55 out of 100. This is a working pick: the corrected scorecard ties, and the week 1 shop survey is the pre-registered gate that confirms or reverses it before any side-specific code is written.

## Why
Four tie-breakers survived cross-examination and the red teams.

First, Option 2 has no payer other than the household. Shop software comparables cost ₱5.6k to 19k a month and are called expensive, the one PH entrant launched free, and no PH willingness-to-pay survey exists [MKT §1b, §5]. Option 2 therefore lives on commission from household orders and must acquire households anyway, while also onboarding shops as price-setting partners. Its claim that shops bring their existing pickup customers collapsed: routing a suki through the app costs the shop ₱18 of a ₱150 wash and the household ₱47 extra, and no count of such customers exists [INT §A, §H, §K].

Second, the panel's constraint. Option 1 sells to one party and buys from vendors; Option 2 sells a tool to shops and collects from households, which is a marketplace again. Supplier recruiting and quality visits are real founder work under Option 1, so this is a difference of degree, but it is the only reading of "pick one side" that does not re-admit the other side.

Third, precedent shape, stated honestly. The three concierge survivors (Rinse, Laundryheap, Poplin) set the customer price and outsource washing, at 25 to 30% take rates with staffed supplier management; no shop-priced commission marketplace was found; the shop-side survivors are subscription software [MKT §1a, §1b]. "None found" is not "none survived", and Option 1 at a ₱22 wholesale on a ₱25 list is a 12% spread, half the take of every survivor it cites.

Fourth, price control. A brand-set bag gives a floor on small orders and a bounded bill read at booking, where the customer app today says the shop sets the final price with no cap or approval [INT §B, §G]. Option 2 can approximate both with a cap, the deferred B7 approval, a scale photo and the shop's own minimum, so this is a lever Option 1 holds more tightly, not one Option 2 lacks.

## What we reject
From Option 2: that shipped code makes it the smaller change (both need slices; the residue is scored under build cost); that the guard trigger rate measures variance (a 9 kg bag on a 6 kg estimate passes untouched [INT §B]); that the portal is a single-player POS; that variance sits with shop and customer (no liability clause exists, Money-Flow covers the happy path [INT §O]).

From Option 1: that it deletes the surprise bill (the honest claim is a bounded bill [MKT §1c]); that the rider can weigh at pickup; that time to first revenue is shorter; that named-shop booking fragments routes at pilot scale (auto-match already chooses [INT §C]); that ₱65 per order is revenue (it is pre-rider-pay under both [INT §O]).

From the draft verdict: that the shop-side ask is identical under both options. It is comparable only at the ₱25 median; a ₱28 shop nets ₱24.64 under Option 2 and is asked for ₱22 under Option 1 [INT §J; MKT §2a]. And that the pilot can run on Scheduled Piaggio routes: auto-dispatch is Express only and no fleet or run model exists.

## Numbers
Worked order: 6 kg at ₱25 = ₱150 wash, ₱18 commission, ₱132 remit, ₱40 delivery at 0.6 km, ₱7 service, ₱197 total, ₱65 kept before rider pay [INT §A]. At ₱22/kg wholesale both options earn ₱18 on wash; at ₱20/kg Option 1 earns ₱30. Each unbilled kg inside a bag costs ₱22, so an average overage or over-recording of 0.55 kg erases that ₱12 edge. Service fee ₱13 versus ₱7 is ₱6 per order, ₱113k a year at 18,840 orders, 60% of the deck's Y1 loss; Table 4 prints 198 for 150 + 40 + 13 [INT §E, §F]. Primary research: zero customer surveys, zero shop interviews, zero LOIs, zero signed shops [INT §K]. Every threshold below is founder judgement; the packs contain no basis for them.

## Risks
Supplier quality is the documented concierge failure mode and is unstaffed [MKT §1a, §5]. Under a bag price only Wash & Go cares whether the shop records 5.2 or 6.0 kg, and the shop's entry is the only record [INT §B]: a moral hazard Option 2 does not carry. Option 1 can recruit only the cheap tail of a ₱22 to 30 city, and shops are getting busier, not idler [MKT §2b], so wholesale volume may have nothing to buy. A principal seller needs Terms, receipts and a wholesale agreement; both packs are silent. Preference risk runs both ways: the shipped-code argument is sunk cost and the concierge argument favours a rebuild; the panel should discount both.

## Validation plan (4 weeks)
Week 1, zero code: phone 20 shops, visit 5. Record walk-in ₱/kg and minimum, turnaround, idle hours, lowest ₱/kg for 10 or more batched bags a week paid weekly by GCash, yes or no to listing at their own rate with 12% shown openly, and existing pickup customers. Ask the panel whether it meant business model or build scope. Reconcile Table 4, decide rider pay, decide hosting.
Week 2: read the survey. Five or more of 20 at or below ₱22 (three at ₱20), idle hours reported and narrow dispersion confirms Option 1; fewer than five, or five or more preferring their own rate, flips to Option 2. Sign one shop on a wholesale sheet. Close T0.
Week 3: side-specific slices alongside the pilot-ready plan (Option 1: price-list config, remit-only weigh sheet, customer copy, rider door note; Option 2: B7 approval, hard cap, commission exposure, scale photo).
Week 4: founder dry run; invited orders only once the infrastructure gates close. A founder is present with a Wash & Go scale for the first 50 weigh-ins; log estimate kg, shop kg, scale kg, wholesale paid, published price, door outcome.

## Product implications
Decouple customerTotal from washValue behind a flag; add price-list config; treat ratePhp as the wholesale rate; wire minOrderPricePhp, which is inert today. Keep weigh(), the second confirm and the READY_FOR_RETURN gate; switch the portal weigh sheet to remit-only amounts; log actual minus estimate per order. Run the pilot on the Express path with founders batching pickups into windows. Customer app: new copy, Change-laundry hidden, named shop kept on the custody stamp as evidence. Wash-dry-fold only. Landing page: one bag price plus the delivery matrix, not "from ₱X". Legal form of a principal seller is a counsel item before the first order.

## Objections considered
Accepted and corrected: the floor and fee levers are not Option 1 exclusive; "6 kg modal" is a planning assumption; "every survivor", "trending to zero", "none survived" and the resentment band at 12% were overstated; the lead dev roadmap sentence is withdrawn; Mr Jeff is removed from scoring; the take-rate gap, stuffed-bag cost, sticker dispersion, kg moral hazard, busier shops, legal form and founder-judgement thresholds are now in the memo; the Scheduled tier is unbuilt; the ₱40 is a formula base; the portal weigh sheet was omitted from build cost; the four-week order count was not deliverable; the survey is the single experiment.
Partially accepted: the one-funnel claim (degree, not kind; rescored 7 vs 5); Poplin (a brand-price survivor with a brand-enforced procedure, so it narrows bill certainty but is not an Option 2 precedent); the hard-side inference (kept at low weight); liability (refused orders land on the platform under both; moral hazard is Option 1 only).
Rejected: deferring the pick. The panel asked for a side; a working pick with a pre-registered reversal sequences the week 3 code, and no side-specific code runs before the survey is read.

## What to tell the panel
We picked the customer, and the call is close. Wash & Go sells one published price to households and buys washing wholesale from one or two shops; the portal survives as a supplier tool. The pick rests on precedent and market structure, not local data, because we have none yet. This week we survey 20 shops in both frames and have written down in advance the numbers that send us to the shop-side model instead. First logged orders start when our infrastructure gates close, not on a date we cannot keep.

## Red team objections and how they were handled

**Evidence lens** (refuted: True).
- The scorecard margin rests on relabeling, not evidence. Criterion 3 (weight 12, scored 8 vs 4) says Option 1 'sells to one party and buys from vendors' so it has one funnel. But the verdict's own flip condition 1 and Week 1-2 plan require a 20-shop wholesale survey and a signed supplier, and [MKT §1a, §5] show Laundryheap staffs a Partner Operations team with monthly facility reviews. Supplier recruiting and management is a second funnel under Option 1 too; the verdict calls it 'vendor sourcing' and stops counting it. Renaming the shop side does not remove it.
- The 'structural levers Option 2 lacks' are not Option-1-exclusive. The small-order floor: platform-config.service.ts:116 already carries minOrderPricePhp (admin-editable, default 0, [INT §A]) and the code comment at :15 says it has no effect yet, so wiring a floor is identical work under either option; ZC shops already impose minimums themselves (Laundry Bords 5 kg, ejournals 3 kg [MKT §2a]), so under Option 2 the floor can simply be the shop's own minimum. The 'only lever is commissionPct' claim in criterion 2 is false: serviceFeePhp and platformFeePhp ([INT §A :108, :117]) are customer-side levers that exist under Option 2 and do not touch the shop's take.
- Poplin is counter-evidence the verdict cites but does not apply. [MKT §1c] says Poplin charges an estimate at checkout, weighs after washing, and bills actual weight with no cap, and survives at a 25% take rate 'because customers accept a known $/lb'. That is the exact mechanism the verdict scores Option 2 down for in criterion 4 ('bills the latest shop weight with no cap, no approval').
