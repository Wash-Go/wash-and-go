# Evidence pack: concierge brand (Option 1) vs shop-side platform (Option 2)

Compiled 2026-10-25 for the Wash & Go council debate. Web research only; no primary interviews.
Confidence key: **H** = primary source or multiple independent sources agree; **M** = single credible secondary source or dated primary; **L** = aggregator, vendor marketing, or unverified. Anything marked UNVERIFIED could not be confirmed.

Caveat on the whole pack: almost no Philippine-specific data exists on weight disputes, laundry SaaS willingness to pay, or shop utilization. Those gaps are themselves a finding: the panel's "pick a side" question cannot be settled from published evidence alone and needs local shop interviews.

### 1c. How survivors handle weight variance (H)
- **Poplin**: estimate at checkout, weigh dry after wash, charge actual. Works because customers accept a known $/lb and the brand absorbs disputes.
- **Rinse**: per-bag flat price in two sizes; no minimum; charged only after delivery.
- **Laundryheap**: base bag (6 kg / 15 lb) + per-kg overage; functions as a floor price with a predictable step.
- **Hampr**: flat per 13-gallon bag, no scale at all; marketed as "zero surprise fees". Trade press notes by-the-bag "trains customers to wait until bags are stuffed" ([PlanetLaundry](https://planetlaundry.com/?p=11176) M).
- **Small operators**: some weigh at pickup in front of the customer on a digital scale (Hygienic Wash FAQ, L); others invoice after weigh-in.
- No published complaint-rate statistics for laundry weight disputes were found (gap).

## 3. Marketplace strategy: pick one side first

- **Lenny Rachitsky, 17-marketplace study (2019-11-22)**: 14 of 17 (80%) went **supply-first**; exceptions Rover, TaskRabbit, Zillow were never supply-constrained because the work was easy to offer and paid meaningfully. Default assumption: supply is the constraint. "Supply drove demand" (Eventbrite, DoorDash: restaurants bring the demand). [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee) **H**. Direct sales was a lever for ~60% ([Andrew Chen repost](https://andrewchen.com/grow-marketplace-supply/) M).
- **Andrew Chen / a16z, The Cold Start Problem (2021)**: build one **atomic network** in a tiny dense geography (Bank of America launched its card only in Fresno); focus disproportionately on the **hard side (usually supply)**; "come for the tool, stay for the network" (single-player utility first). [Dialogue Review](https://dialoguereview.com/starting-from-cold/) M, [Frog Capital](https://frogcapital.com/think-frog/the-cold-start-problem-by-andrew-chen/) M.
- **Chris Dixon single-player mode / OpenTable**: sold restaurant booking software before any diner existed ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/supply-first-vs-demand-first) M).
- **Casey Winters**: early metrics that matter are supply-to-demand conversion, enough transactions for supply to stick, enough quality for demand to return ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/building-winning-marketplaces-casey-winters/) M).
- **Bill Gurley (secondary)**: "demand is the game; most suppliers are easy to sign up" ([Substack summary](https://richardtoad.substack.com/p/network-effect-in-businesses) L).
- **NFX test**: which side are you more worried about mid-term; where will you spend more time and money ([NFX](https://nfx.com/post/marketplace-expansion-framework) M).
- **YC (Adora Cheung lecture, secondary)**: run the marketplace manually first; pick one metric where both sides win ([echai summary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/first-customers/i-run-a-two-sided-marketplace-do-i-get-my-first-customers-by-starting-with-supply-or-demand-and-how-do-i-avoid-the-empty-restaurant-problem) L).

Application (synthesis, M): with 20,000+ shops, 119 in Zamboanga, competition "tightening", and 81% single-shop owners, **laundry supply in Zamboanga is plentiful and under-utilised, i.e. the Rover/TaskRabbit exception**: supply is not the constraint, demand is. The literature then says the hard side is the customer, and the brand that owns the customer (Option 1) is doing the hard-side work. Option 2 only wins if shops are the hard side, which the density data argues against. Caveat: this is inference, not a cited finding; Lenny's own default is "assume supply".

## 4. Price predictability evidence

- **Baymard Institute**: extra costs at checkout are the #1 reason for abandonment, **39%** of abandoners (slow delivery 21%, forced account 19%). Recommends showing full cost early, or a clear estimate with explanation. [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment) **H** (survey year/sample not on page).
- Other industry figures 19-56% abandonment over unexpected costs; wide spread reflects survey wording ([Databox](https://databox.com/shopping-cart-abandonment-rate), [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/?p=977837), L).
- **Philippines, Milieu Insight 2025 (n=500)**: reported as "58% would abandon carts if surprise charges appear"; "88% open to higher fees if they guarantee on-time delivery"; 89% want marketplaces to hold logistics partners accountable for cost. [Tribune 2025-09-14](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/09/14/online-shoppers-pay-more-for-better-service) M; [Philstar 2025-09-15](https://www.philstar.com/business/2025/09/15/2472780/pinoy-online-shoppers-prefer-timely-delivery-over-savings-study) M (Philstar version confirms 88%, the 58% figure appears only in Tribune/Milieu summaries, M-L).
- **Uber**: company states riders are more likely to request when shown an exact fare than a range ([Uber ANZ blog](https://www.uber.com/au/en/blog/upfront-fares-anz/) M, company claim). Columbia (Sherman) critique says upfront pricing let Uber raise rider prices opaquely ([Medianama 2025-07](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-study-uber-upfront-pricing-profit-driver-loss/) M). Lesson: fixed upfront price is a conversion lever and a margin lever at once.
- **Grab PH**: shows a fixed fare before booking; PCIJ (2024-07-12, 1,328 data points) found **surge on every GrabCar ride, average 1.51x**, and the regulator cannot fully reconstruct the fare. LTFRB matrix ₱45 base + ₱15/km + ₱2/min, surge cap 2x. Customer confusion came from opacity of composition, not from the price changing after the fact. [PCIJ](https://pcij.org/?p=18565) **H**.
- **Angkas**: ₱50 first 2 km, ₱10/km to 7 km, ₱15/km after; **stopped 1.5x surge Sept 2025** under DOTr TWG rules disallowing surge ([Malaya 2025-09-20](https://malaya.com.ph/business-news/angkas-stops-imposing-surge-fees) M). 2023 LTFRB probe: riders bypassing app charged 5-10x the matrix ([Manila Standard 2023-05-16](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/manila-standard/20230516/281771338541713) M). Lesson: PH regulators and riders push toward fixed, matrix-based upfront fares; off-app price drift is the thing customers complain about.
- **Lalamove PH**: DTI demanded explanation when a "December Fee" and surcharges more than doubled rates; DTI official said such charges "are not allowed" ([Unbox.ph](https://unbox.ph/news/lalamove-explains-december-fee-other-surcharges/) M, year unclear).
- **Weight-based disputes**: no laundry complaint statistics found. Analogues: moving industry per-pound estimates without weigh tickets are a classic dispute; courier weight-discrepancy guides recommend photo of parcel on scale as evidence ([iThink Logistics](https://www.ithinklogistics.com/blog/weight-discrepancy/) L). Poplin pre-empts disputes by weighing dry and emailing a receipt (H).

## 5. Supply-side evidence (shops)

- **Pain points (PH, M-L)**: tightening neighbourhood competition, rising utilities, 81% single-shop owners, interest in pivoting to hotel linen for volume (Is It Clean 2026, H). Consultancies cite poor site selection and low foot traffic as common failure causes (L). WeClean closed loss-making branches (M). A DSWD-funded shop in Bayugan earns ~₱6,000/mo (M, tiny sample). **No quantified utilization or idle-capacity data found** for PH shops (gap; filipiknow's "75 kg per machine per day" is a planning assumption, L).
- **Willingness to pay for SaaS**: no PH survey found. Context: only 6% of PH MSMEs use email + website + analytics; 23% use no digital tools (DTI via [Globe](https://www.globe.com.ph/business/insights/leveraging-technology-for-growth-resilience), M). Global laundry SaaS charges $99-335/mo (≈₱5.6k-19k), which reviewers already call expensive for small shops (M). LaundromatAI entering PH with a **free** tier (M) suggests local price expectation is near zero for software alone.
- **Commission tolerance, PH food delivery**: GrabFood 22-25% standard, 26-30% priority, 30-33% express, + 3-8% marketing + 2-3% card; Foodpanda 20-23 / 25-28 / 28-32% + 2-5% marketing (Klikit guide 2026-05-26, **L**: vendor, no sources, conflicts with its own other page citing 12-18%). Documented merchant complaints (Jan 2022): 27% commission + extra 12% since June 2021, up to 39% of revenue in fees, ₱250 weekly deduction, 2-week payouts stretching to 2 months; Grab pays daily. [Unbox.ph](https://unbox.ph/news/foodpanda-philippines-is-allegedly-losing-restaurant-partners-due-to-complaints/) M; [NoypiGeeks 2022](https://www.noypigeeks.com/?p=144915) M. No 2023-2026 PH commission complaint coverage found.
- **Laundryheap** manages partner facilities with monthly reviews on capacity, complaints, payments, quality and audits (job listing, M): the supplier-management overhead of Option 1 is real and staffed.
- **Washmen** abandoned partner laundromats for its own plant once scale exposed quality/capacity limits (M). **Laundrapp** died partly because factories could not match independent-cleaner care (competitor opinion, M). Supplier quality control is the recurring Option 1 failure mode.

## 6. Gaps and what would settle the debate
1. No Zamboanga price survey exists; two datapoints (₱27.75/kg, ₱200/8 kg load). A 20-shop phone survey would be the first real local data.
2. No PH data on shop idle capacity, cash flow, or commission tolerance for laundry. Shop interviews needed.
3. No complaint statistics on laundry weight disputes anywhere; the industry answer is procedural (weigh dry, photo, receipt) or structural (flat bag).
4. Mr Jeff is the only PH precedent for a shop network under one app; its parent failed, its franchisee survives on 3 districts.
5. Option 2's SaaS comparables charge ₱5k+/mo and PH entrants are going free; Option 2 revenue would have to come from commission, which puts it in the 20-30% food-delivery band shops already resent.
