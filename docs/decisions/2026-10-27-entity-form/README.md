Status: **working pick, stock corporation of the three founders now, confidence 63/100**, with a dated stopgap (DTI
sole prop under the CEO) only if the November cash gate fails. General partnership rejected under every scenario.
Founders decide; a Philippine lawyer or accountant confirms the items listed at the end. Nothing here is legal advice.

How this was produced: a fact pack (`entity-facts.md`, every rule and fee with URL, date and confidence grade) was
compiled first. Three advocates (sole prop, partnership, corporation) argued on that pack only, cross-examined
each other, and restated with confidence numbers. A judge (lawyer and CPA persona) ruled for the sole prop as a
stopgap; an accountant red team and a lawyer red team both refuted the draft (cash arithmetic understated the sole

an unregistered partnership under Civil Code Art. 1767). The judge answered every objection and reversed to the
corporation. Raw transcript kept outside the repo.

Ban's framing: "assuming we don't have any capital to put into the business yet." The council's answer: no capital
is not a reason to avoid the corporation. The Revised Corporation Code has no minimum capital; the share subscription
is the company's own retained cash, not a fee; the government-fee delta between sole prop and corporation is
roughly ₱3,000 to ₱7,000. What zero capital does rule out is carrying a form that cannot hold three owners while
three people contribute work and code, because that structure is a partnership in law whether or not it is registered.

# Entity form ruling: Wash & Go, November 2026

## Question
Which form to register now for a three-founder, pre-revenue laundry concierge in Zamboanga City (seller of record, custody of clothes, 2 to 4 riders in a closed December pilot, payments partner in Q1 2027): DTI sole proprietorship under the CEO (A), SEC general partnership (B), or SEC stock corporation (C, OPC as a variant).

## Recommendation
Register C now: an ordinary stock corporation with the three founders as incorporators, authorized capital inside the P2,000 minimum fee band, the smallest cash subscription eSPARC accepts (attempt P5,000 or less in equal thirds), filed by Wednesday 4 November, OneSEC if the industry class fits, Regular Processing if not. The draft ruled A; after the red teams' corrections C wins the scorecard 7.03 to 5.56 (B 4.38). Confidence 63. One gate: the three must hold about P9,000 to P15,000 by 4 November, the nominal subscription counted inside that sum. If not, A is the stopgap under the terms in Risks.

## Why
The cash gap was mis-stated. The LGU and BIR chain is the same under every form; the discriminating cost is the entity step: DTI P530 against SEC P3,100 to P3,600 plus eSECURE P400, DST of about P50 on a P5,000 subscription and notarization P1,500 to P3,000, a delta of P3,000 to P7,000 [FACT §3]. The subscription is the company's retained cash, not a fee [FACT §2].

The deferral premium is scheduled, not contingent. The brief plans a payments partner in Q1 2027 and every provider needs a 2303 (pilot plan line 220). A then C is P14,500 to P25,000 against P9,000 to P15,000 for C now, a premium of P5,500 to P10,000 [FACT §3, §6].

The liability event is December, not payments go-live. One rider collision is not scaled by order count; under A, Art. 2180 and bailment claims reach the CEO's own assets, under C they stop at corporate assets absent fraud [FACT §5]. The pack is silent on insurance; the form is the only shield in evidence.

A's founders' agreement is legally fragile. Three people pooling expenses, co-managing and sharing future profit under one DTI name fit the elements of Arts. 1767 and 1772 [FACT §2]; read as a partnership, it puts Art. 1816 and Arts. 1822 to 1824 exposure on the co-founders without a record, and Sec. 22(B) taxes it as a corporation anyway [FACT §4, §5].

Tax does not decide it. In the pilot A on the 8% option pays zero and C pays 3% percentage tax on gross and no income tax on the deck's Y1 loss [FACT §4; evidence §G]; in a loss year A on 8% pays more than C above about P400,000 gross (my arithmetic).

Timing fits. Filed 4 November, Regular eSPARC gives a COI by about 13 November, worst case 25 November with hard copies; BIR and the Mayor's permit then run in parallel, the same tail A faces [FACT §1, §9].

Per founder: the CEO gains a liability wall and a cap table, gives up sole control and the 0% band; the other two get recorded shares with vesting for a nominal subscription and assignment of their work, and take officer duties with own-act liability.

## What we reject
B: corporate tax with solidary tort liability on all three, slowest entity step, dissolves to admit the angel [FACT §4, §5, §6]. OPC: one owner, mandatory conversion within 60 days of any share transfer [FACT §8]. A now: kept only as a cash stopgap.

## Numbers
- All-in: A P5,000 to P8,000; B P7,000 to P12,000; C P9,000 to P15,000; entity-step delta A to C P3,000 to P7,000 [FACT §3].
- A then C premium: P5,500 to P10,000 (A sunk plus BIR closure P500 to P2,000) [FACT §3, §6].
- Tax: individual 0% to P250,000 then 8% on gross; corporation 20% on net plus 10% on dividends; 3% percentage tax if non-VAT under any form [FACT §4]. At P197 per order the P250,000 band ends near 1,269 orders and VAT starts near 15,228, below the deck's Y1 18,840 (evidence §G).

## Risks

- Alter ego: every peso through corporate books from day one; no COD on a personal GCash.
- Calendar: GIS, 1702 series, SEC FS or SMR, DST by 5 December; late SEC filings P1,000 to P10,000 per form per month (L) [FACT §4].
- Stopgap A, if the gate fails: a counsel-cleared pre-incorporation agreement (IP licensed to the CEO personally, option for shares at incorporation, no profit split or common fund before it); incorporate before any provider application or first non-invited customer and by 31 March 2027; graduated rates unless conversion is certain before P250,000; the CEO alone bears Art. 2180 and bailment risk.

## Draft verdict and why it was reversed

The draft ruled for the sole prop (confidence 60): Register option A now: a DTI sole proprietorship under the CEO, City of Zamboanga scope (P530 entity step, same day), with a notarized founders' agreement signed the same week. Incorporate an ordinary stock corporation of the three (not an OPC) at the earliest of four triggers: (1) a written, dated angel commitment conditional on shares; (2) the decision to apply to PayMongo or Maya, so provider and bank onboarding happen once, on the entity that will keep running; (3) trailing gross sales reaching about P200,000 as seller of record (about 1,000 orders at the P197 worked bill, inside the P250,000 band); (4) 30 June 2027 as a backstop date. Conversion cost, stated: a new entity, not a filing: P9,000 to P15,000 SEC, DST and notary for the corporation, plus P500 to P2,000 BIR closure of the sole prop, plus P2,000 to P4,000 of invoices made obsolete, plus a new Mayor's permit; several weeks; about P8,000 to P14,000 more than incorporating now [FACT §3, §6]. The ruling flips to C now if the founders can pool roughly P12,000 to P15,000 plus a nominal share subscription by mid-November, or if the angel commits in writing before registration.

**Accountant lens** (refuted: True).
- The structure the verdict builds is an unregistered partnership wearing a sole-prop name. Step 1 has three people sign a notarized agreement under which two contribute property and industry (the design system and the codebase under licence, their ongoing work), all three co-manage ('decisions needing all three'), expenses are pooled as founder loans credited to future shares, and profits are divided by a formula at a fixed event. FACT §2 cites Civil Code Arts. 1767 and 1772: a partnership arises when persons bind themselves to contribute money, property or industry to a common fund with intent to divide profits, and one with P3,000 or more of capital must be recorded with SEC. The codebase alone (42 API spec files, 434 unit tests, evidence §M) is property worth far more than P3,000. If that reading holds, the two co-founders already carry Art. 1816 pro rata subsidiary liability for contracts and Arts. 1822 to 1824 solidary liability for a rider tort [FACT §5], without any of B's record, and the agreement's 'co-founders are not partners or agents' clause is exactly the stipulation Art. 1817 makes void as to third parties [FACT §5]. The verdict lists this only as a flip condition and misstates that Art. 1767 is not in the pack. Under the lawyer lens it is the probable reading of the verdict's own design, and it collapses the liability score (criterion 3) and the tax score (criterion 6), because NIRC Sec. 22(B) taxes any partnership 'no matter how created or organized' as a corporation [FACT §4], so A's 0% band and 8% option would not even be available to the enterprise.
- The cash criterion that decides the near tie (A 6.65 vs C 6.61) is overstated. The brief says 'a few thousand pesos of government fees at most'; A's own all-in is P5,000 to P8,000 plus notary [FACT §3], so A does not fit the brief either. The LGU and BIR chain (barangay, Mayor's permit P2,000 to P4,000, DST P30, invoices P2,000 to P4,000) is common to every form. The only discriminating cash item is the entity step: P530 for DTI versus roughly P3,100 to P3,600 SEC plus DST at 1% of a nominal subscription plus eSECURE P400 [FACT §3], with notarized hard copies deferrable up to one year under OneSEC [FACT §1]. That is a delta of about P3,000 to P5,500, not the 9-versus-4 gap the scorecard awards at weight 25. Re-score that one criterion honestly and C wins the weighted total.
- Trigger (2) is not a contingency, it is a decision already taken. The brief says the company 'plans an online payments partner (PayMongo or Maya) in Q1 2027', and pilot plan D2 holds the provider behind a flag only until DTI/SEC and the 2303 exist. The verdict's own rule is that the PayMongo or Maya application must never be made on the sole prop, so incorporation is forced within two to five months of the DTI filing. The deferral premium (P8,000 to P14,000 by the verdict's arithmetic, P8,000 to P16,000 by mine: A P5,000 to P8,000 plus notary P500 to P2,000 plus C P9,000 to P15,000 plus closure P500 to P2,000 plus a second Mayor's permit P2,000 to P4,000, against C now P9,000 to P15,000) is therefore the base case, not an option value, and criterion 8 ('paid only when a trigger fires') scores it wrongly. The founders would spend roughly P17,000 to P31,000 over six months to avoid spending P9,000 to P15,000 in November.
- The claim that 'the UI/UX lead and the lead developer carry no personal liability during the pilot' is stated as known but is an inference, and the verdict's own indemnity clause contradicts it. Under A 'the business' is the CEO herself [FACT §5], so an indemnity from the business to the CEO is an indemnity from herself to herself; the only persons who could actually indemnify her for an Art. 2180 rider judgment or a bailment loss are the two co-founders personally. Either the indemnity is empty (and the CEO's 'unlimited, alone' exposure is understated because she has no recourse) or it is real (and the co-founders have personal liability after all). The verdict cannot have both.
- Riders and contracts under A are personal to the CEO and do not move cleanly to the corporation. The pilot plan says the company hires or contracts riders, dispatches them by app, caps their outstanding COD at P1,500 and sets their pay model (evidence §A, §O; pilot D3, D4): the control-test indicators FACT §5 names. If riders are employees, the CEO must register personally as employer with SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG, and FACT §5 says those liabilities attach to the responsible person regardless of form. At conversion, rider agreements, partner laundry agreements and customer Terms signed by the CEO must be novated to the corporation with each counterparty's consent, riders re-registered under a new employer number, and the counsel-drafted acceptance screens (pilot plan gates: 'Partner laundry agreement + rider agreement drafts, Terms/Privacy final') re-papered. The verdict's sequence lists 'transfer contracts' as one line and prices none of it.
- Tort exposure is sized by frequency when it should be sized by severity. The verdict calls the pilot exposure 'small and short' because of invited customers, COD and 2 to 4 riders. A single motorcycle or e-bike collision with a pedestrian is not scaled by order count; Art. 2180 vicarious liability lands on the CEO's personal assets in A and stops at corporate assets in C absent fraud [FACT §5]. The pack is silent on insurance, so the practical shield the verdict relies on is unverified; the only shield in the pack is the corporate form itself.
- The verdict concedes C's shield 'pays off at public launch and at payments go-live' but places the incorporation trigger at payments, not at the first rider on the road. The exposure event is December (riders dispatched, clothes in custody), the trigger is Q1 2027. If the verdict believes its own concession, the trigger is one quarter late.
Factual errors claimed:
- 'Civil Code Art. 1767 is not in the pack': it is. FACT §2 cites 'Civil Code Arts. 1767, 1772' for the rule that partnership capital may be money, property or industry. The verdict's flip condition 5 is built on a false premise of silence.
- 'Only A fits [a few thousand pesos]': A's all-in is P5,000 to P8,000 plus a notarial fee [FACT §3], above the brief's ceiling; the LGU and BIR chain is common to all forms, so the real discriminator is the entity-step delta of roughly P3,000 to P5,500, not the whole-chain figures the scorecard compares.
- 'The UI/UX lead and the lead developer carry no personal liability during the pilot' is presented as fact under the 'Per founder' heading; the pack supports only that a sole prop has one owner and that side agreements create 'tax and labor ambiguity' [FACT §5, §6]. Given Arts. 1767, 1816, 1817 and 1822 to 1824 in the pack, it is an assumption and should be labelled one.
- 'The premium is paid only when a trigger fires, at which point angel cash or pilot revenue should exist': trigger (2) is the Q1 2027 payments-provider decision the brief states as already planned, so the premium is a scheduled cost, not a contingent one; criterion 8 and decisive argument 6 treat it as optional.
- 'The business, and the corporation once formed, indemnifies the CEO': under A there is no business separate from the CEO [FACT §5]; the clause as drafted is circular unless the co-founders personally indemnify her, which contradicts the 'no personal liability' claim.
- 'IP licence from the UI/UX lead and the lead developer to the sole prop': a sole proprietorship has no separate personality [FACT §5]; the licensee is the CEO in her own name, and the licence is hers personally on departure, incapacity or death.
- The memo and scorecard cite Sec. 73(D) constructive receipt as settled; the pack labels it M, quoted from knowledge, with the text not fetched (Gap 9). The verdict upgrades an M fact to H.
- 'OPC ... the same OneSEC eligibility gap': the comparison table and §8 of the pack state OPC is OneSEC-eligible; only the laundry industry-class question (Gap 2) is shared. Minor, since OPC is rightly rejected on other grounds.

**Lawyer lens** (refuted: True).
